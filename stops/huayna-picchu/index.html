The peak behind the postcard

Huayna Picchu (Wayna Picchu / Huaynapicchu) is the sharp summit that rises directly behind Machu Picchu in classic photos. The hike is short in distance but steep, exposed, and slow when wet or crowded.

Our registry lists about 8,642 ft (2,634 m) for the summit area — a climb from the citadel, not from sea level, but still a leg-burner after four trail days.

Permits and limits

Huayna Picchu is not included in a basic Machu Picchu entry. You need a specific timed ticket with a daily quota. Recent tourism explainers often cite on the order of ~350 climbers per day split across morning entry windows — verify the current official number and schedule before you book, because caps and product names change.

Difficulty

  • Steep stone stairs, narrow sections, exposure
  • Cables or handlines may appear on harder pitches depending on maintenance era
  • Poor choice if you have severe fear of heights, unstable knees, or mobility limits
  • Budget multiple hours round-trip including queues — not a “quick 20 minutes”

Temple of the Moon / Great Cavern

Older trek blurbs sometimes imply the Temple of the Moon is a casual add-on beyond the summit. In recent ticket systems it is often a separate route/product (and may be seasonal). Do not assume a Huayna summit ticket includes the cavern/Moon Temple — read the exact product you purchase.

GPS / tour note. Huayna Picchu is included as a tour stop for spatial context in the 3D experience (#huayna-picchu). Summit access still depends on your park ticket, not the virtual tour.

Practical tips

  • Book early in high season — quotas sell out.
  • After an Inca Trail finish, be honest about fatigue before stacking Huayna the same day.
  • Gloves help on steep stone; water and sun protection are mandatory.
  • Turn around in electrical storms.
  • Do not schedule a razor-thin train after a slow summit.

Notes on sources

Difficulty character is stable across guides. Daily caps, circuit codes, and Moon Temple bundling are regulatory details — re-check official sales channels at trip time.
